# Fidenne Privacy

This page says **what Fidenne keeps on your Mac, what it sends off it, and what it does not collect at all**.
Technical details such as file names and addresses are collected at the very end, under "Technical details".

**What reaches the developer is only the problem reports and diagnostics you send yourself.** Everything else (listening history · settings · lyrics · translations · accounts) stays on this Mac or goes directly, and only, to the services you turned on (Apple Music · Spotify · YouTube Music · lyrics providers · the AI provider you chose — some of them overseas companies). To ask something, use the app's **Help › Create diagnostics for a problem report…** or a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/fidenne/fidenne/issues).

Reference version: <!-- gen:version -->0.7.0<!-- /gen --> · last updated 2026-10-31

- Your audio is neither stored nor sent.
- Information about the song you are listening to leaves only when you use a feature such as lyrics, search, or an integration, and only to the places listed below.
- There is no analytics, advertising, or error-reporting tool in the app, and nothing is logged remotely.

---

## 1. Audio

Fidenne receives audio, processes it in your Mac's memory, and sends it to your DAC. **The audio is neither stored nor transmitted off your Mac.**
There is no recording feature and no "save the current song" feature. The audio is handled briefly in memory only to play it and apply effects, and the screen shows status figures such as output format and level. There is no way to send audio over the internet (no streaming, upload, or cloud backup).
Fidenne does not provide music or service access rights, and it has no feature that lifts access restrictions or technical protection measures. Each music service's own terms of use still apply.

### External audio plug-ins (Audio Units)

Turning on an external audio plug-in passes the sound that is playing, **inside this Mac's memory**, to the separate process that plug-in runs in. A plug-in is a separate program made by another developer, so its behaviour follows that developer's terms and policies — it may write files, and it may connect to the internet for a licence or update check, and Fidenne can neither see nor stop that.
Fidenne writes no audio to disk, but a plug-in can. **Fidenne sends nothing because of plug-ins**, and the table under "What leaves your Mac" gains no row. A plug-in that is not turned on receives no sound. When the plug-in list is built, though, Apple's validation tool (auval) opens each installed plug-in in turn; it uses a test signal, not the sound that is playing.

## 2. What stays on your Mac

All of this is files on your Mac and none of it is sent anywhere. Files the app creates can be read and written only by your account.
File names and locations are under "Technical details".

- **A record of what you listened to** — song title · artist · album · time · which service, plus the sound settings at the time (output mode · sample rate · filter · whether EQ etc. were on · the output device's product name · dropout count) and where you played it from (playlist · album · search, etc.).
  For local files in your library, only an unreadable fingerprint is kept instead of the path. You can turn it off in settings, and clear it with **Clear listening history** on the Listening History page.
  Records you erase on Home are only hidden from the screen; they remain in the statistics and in the history file.
  Spotify listening records are kept for seven days, and older records are deleted daily. To keep earlier listening times accurate, a playback boundary time may remain without any song, identifier or service information.
  Spotify playback stays in the plain recent history, but is excluded from listening counts, time and artist statistics, the listening summary on Home, and "Songs you used to listen to often".
  **This record and its statistics are yours.** They stay on this Mac and belong entirely to you. Whether you look through them yourself or analyse them to get equipment recommendations is up to you. Fidenne does not receive this record, and neither asks for it nor needs it to fix or add features. Any statistics Fidenne may collect in the future, with your consent, are only about DSP settings, the experience of using the app and which equipment you listen through (the product name of the DAC or other output device), and contain nothing about which songs you listened to or how much you listened through which service. Fidenne does not compete with music streaming services — it is an audio engine that processes the sound each service's app plays.
- **Resume information** — the last list and song you played, and the position in the song. Turning off or clearing the history removes it too.
- **Remembered sample rate per song** — the rate an Apple Music song was played at, so the next play can start at that rate.
- **Album covers, kept temporarily** — shrunken covers of songs in your history, kept in macOS's Caches folder for quick display; removed 30 days after last use. Covers and information fetched from Spotify are kept on this Mac for 30 days from the day they were fetched (showing them again does not extend this) and are hidden for four days when you disconnect Spotify, then deleted. Reconnecting within that time restores retained data; “Erase everything now” deletes it immediately after confirmation. Pictures from song search and charts are never written to a file; they stay in memory while the app runs.
- **Headphone measurement · target curves** — copies of the public measurement curves your saved EQ uses.
- **Lyrics lookup cache** — lyrics that were found are kept, and removed after 30 days unread. They are stored so that the folder listing alone does not reveal what you listened to.
- **Saved lyrics and translations (Lyrics tab)** — Saved copies in the Lyrics tab are kept on this Mac without expiry. They contain the LRCLIB record number, title, artist, original lyrics and translations. Service labels, save times and last-heard times are also kept for separate service lists and recent-first sorting. Spotify track IDs and names provided by Spotify are not stored here. For information sent during external AI translation, see “AI lyrics translation” below; the provider’s own policy governs its processing and retention. Service labels and hearing times are kept in separate files from lyric bodies. Lyrics tab › Keep › Delete listening associations deletes them independently while keeping lyrics and translations. When an original changes, the previous original and its translations remain together in history until you delete them, whether edited or not. Lyrics tab › Keep › Delete clears saved tab copies and translation stores; it does not clear the lyrics lookup cache.
- **Library** — the folders and playlists you added, and the paths · tags · length · album art of the music files in them; several spellings of a song's names and which songs in different services are the same song (including Apple Music · Spotify track numbers). Spellings the Apple Music catalogue gave are not stored here, and what earlier versions had fetched through the Spotify Web API was removed once. No audio · listening times · accounts.
- **YouTube Music** — imported playlists (the picked CSV file's path and your account are not kept, and no request is made), and, while the browser-extension connection is on, songs played for 5 seconds or more in Chrome · Edge and the lists of playlist · library screens you opened.
  Album art · playback position · queue · volume · liked status stay in memory and are not written to a file. Your account and other pages you visit are not seen. Turning the connection off does not delete received lists; delete them in Library.
- **DSP helper history** — the latest 20 answers received with “Get samples” and the request text you wrote for each (up to 200 characters). Kept on this Mac only, never sent, and can be deleted one by one or all at once.
- **Settings, presets, ABX results** — filter · EQ · crossfeed · UI scale, presets you named, and the results of comparison tests (no songs or devices). Comparison tests are hidden in this beta, so no new results are made. You can reset them in the app.
- **A restore note** — before changing your DAC's sample rate, the app writes down the original value so the next launch can put it back even after a sudden quit. It is removed once restored.
- **The “Fidenne 재생 대기열” and “Fidenne 재생 대기열 2” playlists in Music** — Fidenne alternates between them and fills the current track and the whole remaining list in order. A notice asks once before first use, and other playlists are not touched.
  If iCloud library is on, Apple syncs it to your other devices. You can turn it off under Settings › Privacy › Apple Music.
- **Spotify connection information** — only if you connected, and in this Mac's login keychain rather than app files (it is not synced to iCloud Keychain; Fidenne reads it without asking, and macOS asks you first if another program tries to read it). When you choose to disconnect, tokens and login information are deleted immediately. Spotify listening history, songs in Fidenne lists and likes, song links, resume records, lyric hearing associations and covers are hidden on this Mac and unused for four days. Reconnecting within four days restores retained data. After the deadline, deletion runs at launch before data is shown, every hour while the app is running, and on wake. If the app is closed, deletion runs at its next launch. Settings shows the scheduled deletion time; “Erase everything now” deletes the data immediately after confirmation. An authentication failure or other unrequested connection loss does not schedule deletion.
- **Diagnostic data for problem reports** — numeric records such as queue latency · dropouts · rate · error codes. The distribution build asks once on the first-run screen as an optional item, and starts recording at launch only if you agreed (you can change it under Settings › Privacy).
  A **diagnostic bundle** is made only when you press "Create diagnostics for a problem report…". It holds numbers and true/false values, services and times, a random marker for each song, and lyrics lookup results (whether lyrics were found, and whether LRCLIB had a record — not the record number, since the number alone identifies the song). A marker is the same for the same song only within that one export and cannot be turned back into a song name. It does not hold song titles · artists · albums, lyrics · translations · API keys · file paths · device names · accounts · system logs.

- **In-app event record** — numeric codes record track and service changes, artwork steps, output-device requests and results, audio interruptions, applied settings, browser-command timing and slow UI responses. Even without diagnostic consent, records for the two minutes before and thirty seconds after a problem, launch completion and the last page, and counts of lost records may stay on this Mac for up to seven days. Without consent the limit is 16 MiB, twenty automatic incidents and ten user markers; with consent a continuous record is also kept within 64 MiB and the same time and incident limits. Old records are removed at the first limit reached. No titles, artists, albums, paths, addresses, device names, accounts, error text, stacks or memory contents are recorded; tracks and devices use per-run numbers. Settings › Privacy › **Delete All Records** deletes them. Withdrawing consent deletes continuous records and keeps only problem windows within 16 MiB.
  When you create a diagnostic bundle, what the app caught is listed, and turning an item off leaves it out together with the records around it. The numbers in a bundle are replaced again with random markers made new for each export, so one bundle cannot be joined with another.
  Earlier versions kept a "now playing" list with song titles · artists · albums in the diagnostics folder. This version does not carry that file over; it deletes it and now writes numbers only, without song names.

Deleting the matching file or folder on your Mac removes any of these, and the app recreates it when needed.

To find out the sample rate of an Apple Music song, while the app is running it reads only the sample-rate and format numbers from the lines the Music app leaves in the macOS log. The log lines themselves are neither written nor sent; only the numbers are kept, in the per-song memory and the listening history.
On a non-administrator account macOS blocks only the fast method, and this detection carries on by checking the song's information once a second.

## 3. What leaves your Mac

Audio does not leave. But **what you are listening to can.** Only when you use that feature, and only to the places below. Each place can be turned off separately in settings, ordinary destinations are asked about together on one screen at first run, while AI lyrics translation asks separately when you enable the feature.
With everything off, playback · sound processing · DAC output and local playback carry on as before; only the integration features that use that place turn off.

- **Lyrics** (LRCLIB) — when first needed, you are told what is sent and asked for consent; nothing is sent before that. If you decline, only a `.lrc` file you connected yourself is used.
  Including information about the currently playing song read from the Spotify app, only four things are sent: song title · artist · album · length (for local files too, never the file path or contents). A lookup happens only after you have listened to the same song for 5 seconds or more.
- **Apple Music** — if you have allowed Apple Music access, the song title and first artist name etc. go to Apple for reading the library and finding other spellings or the same song (with the user token Apple issued). Album name and length do not go. The answer (other spellings · the same song) is used in memory only, while the app runs, to find lyrics and link the same song for tracks heard in Apple Music · Spotify · YouTube Music, and is not stored. Only the link saying they are the same song is kept.
  When you press “Save to library” (라이브러리에 저장) in an album's header, that album's Apple Music catalogue number and the user token Apple issued go to Apple, and the album is added to your iCloud library (MusicKit sends it). It is sent only when you press it, and a moment later the app reads the library a few times to check that the album arrived. No song title, album name or artist is in this request, and the app holds no key. There is no feature to take a saved album out from the app.
- **Spotify** — only if you connected, search terms · track and device identifiers · sign-in information go to Spotify. The Client ID is your own developer-app key that you enter yourself, and the app contains no key or token. Without it only the Spotify features turn off and the app works normally.
  **Information received through the Spotify connection (Web API) — playlists, library, search results — is not sent to other services or used for song matching.** Separately, the title, artist, album and length of the currently playing song are read from the Spotify app. This information is used for this Mac's plain recent history and, if you consented to online lyrics search, sent to LRCLIB to find lyrics. Spotify playback is excluded from listening statistics. Neither the audio nor the song information of Spotify songs goes into AI. Spotify is a third-party beneficiary of this privacy policy.
  Information received through the Spotify Web API is used only on Spotify screens. This includes the “Lists provided by Spotify” tab (「Spotify 에서 제공한 목록」) on the listening history page. When you open this tab or change its type or time range, it requests your affinity rankings and recently played list from Spotify. These responses are processed in memory only while the screen is displayed and are not written to files.
  They are neither merged into Fidenne's own listening history or statistics nor exported, and are discarded when you leave the screen, change accounts, or disconnect. When the player did not give the cover of a Spotify song shown on Home, only those song numbers are collected (up to 50 per request) and asked of Spotify once, and the cover is downloaded from the picture address in the answer. These covers are kept in memory only while the app is open and are never written to a file. If Spotify answers that there are too many requests, the app waits before asking again and gives up after a few refusals.

- **Store search · recommendations · album art · headphone measurement lists** — at most search terms or the headphone name you chose go to that service; song information does not. A store search is sent at most once every 4 seconds, and the results stay in memory until the app quits.
- **Saving to YouTube Music** — Fidenne sends no request to YouTube Music or Google. When you press “YouTube Music에 저장” (save to YouTube Music), the app gives the extension a command and the extension presses the save control, once, on the YouTube Music album or song page that is **already open** in your browser. It does not read or bring the sign-in cookie or token to the app; only the result (saved · already saved · a reason such as not found) comes back to the app inside this Mac. The saving itself is something your browser does with YouTube Music.
- **Update check** — when you press "Check for Updates…", and, while automatic checking is on, every 4 hours (and once more at launch and on wake if the last check is older than 4 hours), it reads a new-version notice file from GitHub. Automatic checking is on from the start if you leave the GitHub connection on at first run, and you can turn it off on its own in the network destination settings. A new version is downloaded and installed only when you press, after its signature is verified.
  What goes out is the app version and your IP address; no song · device · settings or system information. The developer does not receive these requests.
- **Received on your Mac only** — there is a temporary doorway that receives the Spotify sign-in result in your browser, and one that receives YouTube Music song information from the Fidenne extension in Chrome · Edge. Neither leaves your Mac, and they are open only while you sign in, or while you have the extension connection on (off by default).
  The **Safari extension** is inside the Fidenne app and is turned on by the same switch. The app and the extension talk only through a socket in a private folder of the same app group (only connections signed by the same developer are accepted), and no song information is written to a file. Nothing leaves your Mac.
  Over the extension connection, album art is fetched and shrunk by your browser from the image servers the page already uses; the app makes no separate request to an image address.
- **How the browser extension uses information (Limited Use)** — Information received by the Fidenne extensions for Safari, Chrome and Edge is used only to tell Fidenne on this Mac what is playing now and pass the commands you press to the YouTube Music page. It is not sold, used for advertising, or passed to anyone else. The Safari extension is included in the Fidenne app. The Chrome extension’s use of information follows the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

If a server redirects to another address, it is followed only if that address is allowed and its switch is on; otherwise the request ends. A request to a place not in the list is treated as a defect and fixed.
There is one exception. For update checks and new-version downloads the app checks only the first address; any redirect after that is followed by the update framework (Sparkle) itself, so the app cannot check it again. GitHub usually redirects these requests to `release-assets.githubusercontent.com`, listed in the table above. A downloaded new version is installed only after its signature is verified.
External links inside the app open in your browser when clicked; they are not requests the app sends.

### AI lyrics translation (optional)

Translating with your own API key has no provider selected and is off by default. Choose Anthropic Claude, OpenAI or Google Gemini, save your own API key, read the selected provider's outgoing-data disclosure and privacy policy, then press “Enable.” General network acknowledgement and key saving alone do not enable it. You can turn it off or withdraw each provider's purpose consent in Settings.

Only original lyric rows, title, artist and execution parameters (target language, model, row IDs and output format) go directly to the chosen provider. The key goes only in its authentication header. No audio, album, file paths, listening history, account identifiers or player identifiers are included. Spotify songs use only LRCLIB-returned lyrics, title and artist; the next song is never pre-translated. The developer does not receive or collect these requests or translations.

Safeguards: when the daily cap or the monthly cap (by default 20 times the daily cap; it can be turned off) is reached, the app says so on screen and continues with on-device translation. At 80 % of a cap it says so once. A failed request is never retried automatically, a stored translation is reused, and asking again for the same song and edition within 10 minutes sends nothing. After 3 provider errors in a row, AI requests stop for the rest of the day. We recommend setting a monthly spending limit in your provider's console. With AI translation enabled and translation lines visible, a request is made after the track settles; connection tests and retries also make requests. Charges from the AI provider are settled between you, the owner of the key, and the provider, and request counts and charges include per-track requests made while translation is enabled. The developer does not bear the charges for requests you pressed. This does not apply when an error in the app sends requests you did not press.

API charges apply to your own account. There is no default daily allowance; an optional user-selected limit is 1–1000 requests. Today's and the last 30 days' counts are not a bill estimate. Connection tests send a fixed synthetic sentence instead of a song and can count toward usage and charges. There is one local request slot, zero automatic retries and a 30-second deadline. Failure falls back to system translation or original text, without switching providers. AI translation rejects every redirect, including same-origin redirects.

Translations made with your API are kept separately on this Mac until you choose “Delete this translation” or “Delete all AI translations.” Turning AI off, deleting a key or withdrawing consent does not erase them. At 256 MiB the app refuses new editions and tells you; it does not evict older ones. Today's and the last 30 days' counts and failed-attempt fingerprints are separate, so deleting translations does not reset usage. Keys are kept in one non-synchronizing login Keychain item per provider, with save, replace and delete in Settings. Keys, lyric text and translations are excluded from this feature's logs and diagnostic bundles.

You check the rights needed to submit content and use results. Providers process and may retain inputs and outputs under their policies and your account settings. Applicable Gemini unpaid terms may permit product improvement and human review; OpenAI's `store:false` does not eliminate abuse-monitoring records. Withdrawing consent cannot recall sent requests; local deletion does not delete provider records.

**The Apple model on this Mac.** “Translate on this Mac (Apple Intelligence)” in Settings needs no key and is used only on macOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence turned on. When it translates, **the lyrics do not leave your Mac**: there is no new destination and no charge. Even with no provider chosen it is used first, automatically, when it is available (which is why this route has no separate consent window). Translations made this way are kept on this Mac and deleted under the same rules as above. macOS downloads the model, which takes storage space, and translating uses this Mac's memory and processing for a few seconds. If it does not work, macOS translation is shown, and last the original.

### AI DSP helper (optional)

The “DSP helper” on the DSP page uses the same API key and provider as lyrics translation. The first time you use it, it shows you exactly what will be sent and asks for consent; having agreed to lyrics translation does not turn it on.

It sends to the provider you chose, once, only when you press “Get samples.” What is sent is the text where you wrote the sound you want, the names, ranges and current values of the sound-processing features, your current EQ, and the names of the headphone, the measurement-rig family and the target you picked.
If you use an external plugin, its name and its controls (name · unit · range · current value) are sent too.
The key is sent only in the authentication header.
**Audio, the title · artist · album of the song you are playing, anything that came from Spotify, file paths, accounts and the names of your saved presets are not sent.** What you type in the request box goes exactly as you typed it.

The three setting samples that come back are shown only after the app checks them against its ranges and safety limits. Only the one you pick after listening is applied; the app never applies one by itself. A failed request is not resent, and requests are counted together with lyrics translation.
The latest 20 requests and answers stay on this Mac only, are never sent anywhere, and a stored sample is checked again against your settings of the moment when you open it. You can delete them one by one or clear them all.
Without a key you can copy the request text, paste it into the AI you use yourself, and paste its answer back into the app. Fidenne sends nothing on this route, and a pasted answer goes through the same checks.

## 4. If you send a problem report

**The app does not send diagnostic bundles.** "Send by mail" only opens your mail app with the recipient, subject and an empty form filled in and attaches no bundle, "Copy mail form" only copies the same text to the clipboard, and "Open GitHub issue composer" only opens a new-post screen in your browser. Attaching and sending a bundle is entirely your own action.

1. A report sent by mail reaches only the developer. It is not made public and is not put in the source repository.
2. Before analysis, the sender's name · mail address · signature are removed. The remaining report and diagnostic bundle (which contains no song names) may be read with analysis tools, including AI tools (such as Anthropic's Claude). Information about songs heard on Spotify is never put into the bundle in the first place.
3. A diagnostic bundle is deleted within 90 days after the problem is fixed. The mail itself stays in the mailbox because a reply is owed.
4. If you want it deleted sooner, tell us at fidenne.support@gmail.com by mail.

## 5. Permissions the app asks for

- **Audio capture** — to receive system sound and convert it to DSD.
- **Audio input (microphone)** — needed when receiving Fidenne Inlet or an input device you chose, because macOS treats virtual input devices like microphones. Only the chosen device is read; the built-in microphone is not opened separately.
- **Apple Events (automation)** — to show the current song of Apple Music · Spotify and control playback.
- **Apple Music library** — for reading the library and for finding other spellings of a played song and the same song in Apple Music (kept in memory; only the link saying they are the same song is kept). If not allowed, it reads through the Music app and does not look up other spellings.

## 6. How to check this yourself

The app code is not published, but the connection addresses, the consent wording and the contents of a diagnostic bundle are not hidden. You can check them yourself as follows.

1. **In the app.** Settings › Privacy › "Network destinations" lists every place Fidenne connects to on the internet and what goes there. In Help › "Create diagnostics for a problem report…" press "Create bundle and view contents", then "Open bundle folder" to open it in Finder and read it yourself before sending anything.
2. **Watching outgoing connections.** Watch Fidenne's connections with a firewall such as LuLu (free, open source) or Little Snitch, and you will see the addresses and times in the "Where data leaves your Mac" table under "Technical details" below. Only the addresses for features you have turned on appear.
3. **What stays on your Mac.** In Finder use Go › Go to Folder… to open the folders in the Technical details file table. If you connected Spotify, you can see the Spotify item in Keychain Access. How to delete each is in the last column of the same table.
4. **Permissions.** System Settings › Privacy & Security shows the permissions you gave Fidenne (the ones listed in section 5: audio capture · microphone · automation · Apple Music library) and lets you turn them off.

## Technical details

This part is for people who want to verify. The promises above are checked on every build by automated checks that scan the source (they do not directly measure behavior while the app runs).
In the audio path's code there are no calls that open a file or write bytes other than five recorded exceptions — the coefficient cache, the library index, and the output-rate restore record (writes; not samples), plus two reads of files you picked — and the app's audio engine processes audio in memory and sends it to the DAC, while the part that draws the screen receives only numbers and state rather than sample buffers.

**Receiving side.** `127.0.0.1` — a loopback listener (port 53682) that receives the Spotify sign-in callback in the browser. It is open only while the user is starting the Spotify connection, and it closes when done. What goes out: nothing — it does not leave the device.

**Receiving side.** `fidenne-browser-bridge/bridge.sock` — a Unix socket (in the user's temporary folder, readable and writable only by the owner) that receives from the Fidenne extension in Chrome · Edge the YouTube Music song information (title · artist · album · duration), the lists of playlist and library screens the user opened, and small album art, and that returns playback controls. The extension, helper, and app exchange only nine fixed commands. It is open only while "Connect with browser extensions (Safari · Chrome · Edge)" under Settings › Privacy › YouTube Music is on (off by default); turning it off closes it and removes the connection file (NativeMessagingHosts). What goes out: nothing — it does not leave the device. Song information is kept only for songs played for 5 seconds, opened lists go to their own files, and album art stays in memory only. The app makes no request to an image address — the extension reads the image inside the browser, without cookies, from the image servers the page already uses (`lh3.googleusercontent.com` · `i.ytimg.com`), and shrinks it.

**Library actions.** Only when you press the control, the extension passes like/unlike actions and saving an album to the library to YouTube Music.

**Receiving side.** `fidenne-safari-bridge/b.sock` — an owner-only Unix domain socket in the app-group folder shared by the Safari extension and Fidenne. Only the extension and app signed by the same team can connect, exchanging fixed commands, song information (title, artist, album and duration), and playback controls. The connection opens while YouTube Music is enabled. Enable the included extension in Safari › Settings › Extensions to connect; this is independent of the Chrome · Edge connection switch. Turning YouTube Music off closes the socket and deletes its file. Nothing leaves this Mac. Song information follows the same rules as for the Chrome · Edge extension.

**Per-item switches.** Each destination can be turned off and on separately, and they are asked about together on one screen the first time you run the app. Even with everything off, capture, DSP, DSD conversion, DAC output, and local playback continue as before — only the integration features that use that destination are turned off. If destinations are added later, the same screen appears once more, showing only the added lines.
